# Limitations, Ethical Considerations & Non-Clinical Disclaimer

## VitalTwin AI — Happiest Health Digital Twin Challenge 2026

### 1. Non-Clinical Research Disclaimer
**CRITICAL NOTICE:** VitalTwin AI is an academic research proof of concept developed specifically for the *Happiest Health Digital Twin Challenge 2026*. It is designed solely for computational evaluation, educational demonstration, and clinical research exploration.

- **NOT A MEDICAL DEVICE:** The software has **not** been cleared, approved, or evaluated by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA), European Medicines Agency (EMA), Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO), or any statutory medical device regulator.
- **NO MEDICAL DECISION MAKING:** Under no circumstances should the probabilities, alerts, trend trajectories, or what-if outputs generated by this system be used to:
  - Prescribe, titrate, or adjust insulin doses.
  - Modify oral hypoglycemic medications.
  - Diagnose diabetes mellitus, prediabetes, or metabolic conditions.
  - Replace direct clinical judgment by a licensed medical practitioner.

### 2. Technical and Modeling Limitations

#### A. Synthetic Domain Gap
The digital twin and predictive model rely entirely on mathematical equations of human physiology (gamma-absorption gut curves, linear activity clearance, and sinusoidal diurnal cycles). While grounded in established endocrinology literature, in silico simulations cannot capture:
- Acute systemic illness, fever, or inflammatory states.
- Medication pharmacokinetics variations (e.g., erratic subcutaneous absorption).
- Psychological acute stress, cortisol surges, and hormonal fluctuations (e.g., menstrual cycle, epinephrine).
- Gastrointestinal dysmotility (gastroparesis) causing delayed carbohydrate absorption.
- Complex mixed-macronutrient meal interactions (fat-protein delayed excursions).

#### B. Interstitial vs. Intravascular Discrepancy
Continuous Glucose Monitors (CGM) measure glucose in interstitial fluid rather than whole blood. In physiological reality, there exists a 5 to 15-minute physiological lag between venous blood glucose and interstitial measurements, particularly during rapid glucose rate of change ($|dG/dt| > 2 \text{ mg/dL/min}$). Although simulated noise includes autoregressive lag, real-world sensor compression artifacts (e.g. pressure-induced sensor attenuation during sleep) and calibration drifts are not fully replicated.

#### C. Machine Learning Horizon and Calibration
- The Random Forest classifier predicts the binary likelihood of crossing a threshold ($\ge 180 \text{ mg/dL}$) within a strict 120-minute window.
- The model evaluates features causally available at time $t$. However, unforeseen behavioral events occurring between $t$ and $t+120\text{ min}$ (e.g. an unplanned snack or sudden high-intensity sprint) will naturally alter actual glucose outcomes relative to the static prediction made at time $t$.
- Held-out evaluation metrics reported in this repository reflect performance on the synthetic data generator. Performance in real-world clinical trials will inherently vary due to higher biological noise and measurement dropouts.

### 3. Ethical and Governance Considerations

- **Autonomous Closed-Loop Restriction:** VitalTwin AI does **not** command physical actuators, insulin pumps, or smart pens. It remains strictly a decision-exploration sandbox for human researchers.
- **Equitable Data Representation:** Synthetic profiles represent three distinct personas with differing ages, BMIs, and baseline severity. When translating to real-world deployment, extensive multi-ethnic, multi-demographic validation is mandatory to prevent biased algorithmic performance.
- **Data Privacy by Design:** Because all records are synthetic, there is zero risk of patient re-identification or privacy violation. Real-world extensions must enforce end-to-end encryption, local differential privacy, and federated learning protocols.
